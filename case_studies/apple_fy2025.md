## 2. Accounting-to-Valuation Bridge

A portfolio analyst should distinguish reported accounting numbers from valuation transformations.

### Free-cash-flow proxy

For this first reproducible case, the model uses:

```text
FCF proxy = cash from operations − capex
          = 111,482 − 12,715
          = 98,767
```

This is intentionally simple. It is not presented as a full unlevered FCFF calculation because the public cash-flow statement does not directly separate every item needed for a textbook FCFF bridge.

### Net cash bridge

The model treats cash plus current and non-current marketable securities as cash-like assets, and commercial paper plus current/non-current term debt as debt.

```text
Cash + securities = 35,934 + 18,763 + 77,723 = 132,420
Debt              = 7,979 + 12,350 + 78,328 = 98,657
Net debt           = 98,657 − 132,420 = −33,763
```

Negative net debt means the company has net cash under this simplified definition.

## 6. Finance Interpretation

### Strengths visible in the reported data

**Scale and profitability.** FY2025 revenue was $416.2B and net income was $112.0B, showing very high absolute earnings capacity.

**Cash generation.** Operating cash flow of $111.5B supports a simplified post-capex FCF proxy of $98.8B.

**Services mix.** Services revenue reached $109.2B, providing a recurring-revenue dimension alongside the hardware ecosystem.

**Balance-sheet flexibility.** Under the simplified cash/securities-versus-debt bridge used here, Apple carries net cash rather than net debt.

### Risks and questions for deeper diligence

**Terminal-value dependence.** A large portion of DCF value comes from cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period, so WACC and perpetual growth deserve scenario testing.

**Growth normalization.** A mature company at Apple's scale cannot be modeled with aggressive growth indefinitely; the forecast deliberately fades growth over time.

**Geographic exposure.** Greater China represented $64.4B of FY2025 sales, making regional demand and operating conditions a material diligence topic.

**Capital-return effects.** Large repurchases can materially change per-share economics and share count over time. A professional model should forecast diluted shares rather than freezing the year-end count.

## 7. What This Case Study Demonstrates

This is not a stock-picking notebook. It demonstrates a finance workflow that a recruiter can inspect:

1. Source reported numbers from company financial statements.
2. Keep reported facts separate from analyst assumptions.
3. Build an accounting-to-cash-flow bridge.
4. Forecast with explicit and reviewable drivers.
5. Run a DCF using reusable Python functions.
6. Stress the valuation with a two-dimensional sensitivity table.
7. Document limitations instead of hiding model simplifications.
